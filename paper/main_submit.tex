%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Quantitative signals and local-language news offer complementary context for multi-agent LLM trading, but their incremental value requires matched evaluation. We study quantitative alpha integration in a five-role framework for Vietnamese equities, combining an 85-candidate OHLCV registry, Vietnamese sentiment and two chart-reading agents. Selected factors and sentiment remain separate inputs to a report-based Decision Agent. A shared-report protocol compares Full and Base on 102 historical market units across nine equities, twelve dates and six blocks, with 25 UP and 77 DOWN net-return labels. Full balanced accuracy is \FullBA\%, versus \BaseBA\% for Base and \LogisticPriceBA\% for a price-only logistic comparator. The Full--Base difference is \DeltaBA\ percentage points, with a descriptive block interval of $[\PrimaryLow,\PrimaryHigh]$ points. Both variants capture seven UP outcomes; Full makes fewer false LONG forecasts. Mean separate-account net returns are \FullReturn\% and \BaseReturn\%, respectively. Three-execution component/placebo panels and train-fit numeric controls provide additional, separately scoped comparisons. The reduced historical scope is exploratory, native factor construction is not fully row-causal, and financial-news validity and availability remain unverified. The study contributes a specified integration and comparative evaluation, while its results do not establish causal component gains or deployable profitability.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Quantitative alpha and Vietnamese sentiment extend a multi-agent trading framework.
\item An 85-factor registry adapts OHLCV signals to single-stock decision context.
\item Shared-report comparisons evaluate factor, sentiment and past-donor variants.
\item Balanced-accuracy gains coincide with fewer false LONG forecasts.
\item Cost-aware accounts distinguish directional scores from economic outcomes.
\end{highlights}
