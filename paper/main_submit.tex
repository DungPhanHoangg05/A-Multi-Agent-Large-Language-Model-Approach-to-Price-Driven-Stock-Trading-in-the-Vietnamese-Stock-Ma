%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used in algorithmic trading, yet knowledge integration across heterogeneous evidence remains underexplored in emerging and non-English markets. We present a five-agent knowledge-fusion framework for financial decision support in Vietnamese equities. The architecture integrates technical indicators, point-in-time quantitative alpha selection, Vietnamese financial sentiment, and vision-language chart analysis, providing a reproducible basis for combining heterogeneous quantitative, textual, and visual evidence in multi-agent reasoning. A paired shared-report protocol isolates the contribution of alpha information while holding upstream evidence fixed, and a four-way ablation separates quantitative-factor and sentiment effects. Across nine equities and 180 paired decisions, the Full System attains 65.0\% accuracy versus 51.1\% without Alpha. The ablation identifies a larger independent contribution from alpha factors than from sentiment, with stock-dependent interactions. An execution-aware long-only ledger nevertheless produces a negative mean net return, showing that improved directional inference does not ensure economic profitability. The framework therefore supports auditable knowledge integration and controlled component evaluation for financial decision support; claims of immediate trading deployment remain outside the available evidence.
\end{abstract}
