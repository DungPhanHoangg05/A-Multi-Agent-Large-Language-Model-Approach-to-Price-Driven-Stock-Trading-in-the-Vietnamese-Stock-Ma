\section{Limitations \& Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations_futurework}

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{L1. Statistical scale and economic interpretation.}
The paired benchmark controls upstream reports and evaluates 180 decisions, but each symbol contributes only 20 observations. Moving-block confidence intervals and exact McNemar tests correctly expose this low per-symbol power: no individual McNemar result is significant at 5\%, even though the cross-symbol Wilcoxon test is significant. The nine tickers are also not a probability sample of the Vietnamese market. Results should therefore be read as evidence for this benchmark universe rather than a market-wide performance estimate.

Directional skill and economic value remain distinct. The Full System raises mean accuracy by 13.9 pp and improves account return relative to No-Alpha for every symbol, yet its mean compounded return is $-1.69\%$ and only three symbols finish positive. Correct SHORT forecasts earn no return because the cash-equity simulator maps SHORT to CASH. Consequently, the reported lift does not establish deployable profitability.

\paragraph{L2. Simplified execution model.}
The fixed-cycle ledger removes the previous horizon mismatch: labels, alpha targets, and account returns all use the same next-open-to-target-close net return, wealth is compounded, and fee plus slippage are charged on both BUY and SELL. Nevertheless, the constants are benchmark assumptions. The model omits time-varying bid--ask spreads, taxes, order-book depth, partial fills, volume-dependent market impact, position limits, and portfolio competition among simultaneous symbols. It also assumes that each $S=L=3$ cycle is closed before the next entry. A venue-calibrated event-driven simulator is required before live deployment.

\paragraph{L3. Alpha selection and sensitivity.}
Selecting five factors from an 85-candidate registry creates multiple-testing and winner's-curse risks. The point-in-time cutoff answers a temporal question---whether future entry and target bars were hidden---but not a statistical-selection question---whether the chosen factor would retain its apparent skill on data unused for choosing its orientation and rank. Here, the same trailing sample supplies the IC sign, the oriented Accuracy and Sharpe diagnostics, and the top-five ranking. Taking $|\mathrm{IC}|$ after sign estimation makes positive and negative sample correlations equally selectable, while selecting the maximum composite scores across 85 candidates increases optimism. No nested cross-validation, Deflated Sharpe Ratio, family-wise/false-discovery correction, or rolling sign-stability threshold was used. The benchmark evaluates the complete adaptive policy at later cutoffs, but its per-factor diagnostics are not unbiased post-selection estimates and should not be interpreted as factor discoveries. The robustness results expose sensitivity rather than resolve multiplicity: FPT produces a negative lift at $W=60$, and performance changes materially across normalisation and weighting schemes.

The 80 WorldQuant-derived entries are also heuristic single-asset translations, supplemented by five proprietary formulas. Replacing a contemporaneous cross-sectional rank with a rolling rank changes both the conditioning set and the economic question. The study does not profile three properties needed to judge whether that translation is stable: (i) an IC-decay curve $\mathrm{IC}_k(h)=\mathrm{corr}_{S}(\alpha^{(k)}_t,r_{t,t+h})$ across horizons $h$; (ii) lagged signal autocorrelation $\rho_k(\ell)=\mathrm{corr}(\alpha^{(k)}_t,\alpha^{(k)}_{t-\ell})$; and (iii) turnover, measured from changes in normalised exposure or signal sign. High autocorrelation can make the effective sample much smaller than the bar count, rapid IC decay can invalidate a three-bar horizon, and high turnover can erase a weak edge after costs. Until these are reported per factor and regime, no claim is made that the adaptations preserve the source factors' decay, capacity, or interpretation.

\paragraph{L4. Hosted-model and visual reliability.}
Temperature zero and fixed prompts do not guarantee bitwise deterministic output from hosted models. Local seeds do not control provider-side decoding, and the archived runs identify provider model aliases rather than immutable weight revisions. The paired design removes upstream variation within a Full/No-Alpha comparison, but separate runs can still differ because of provider updates or inference nondeterminism.

Pattern and Trend agents compare their final visual conclusions with deterministic OHLCV rules and downgrade obvious contradictions. This is a consistency test, not an evidence-faithfulness test. It does not show that the model attended to the named candle, wick, breakout, or trendline; a global-trend shortcut can agree with the rule and pass. Recent candlestick-specific work demonstrates precisely this identification problem and finds strong trend sensitivity with weak or oppositely signed sensitivity to injected local evidence in tested VLMs \cite{wang2026martingale}; broader financial-chart evaluation also reports persistent spatial-reasoning limitations \cite{shu2026finchart}. Because this study contains neither matched counterfactual chart pairs nor human-labelled local-evidence annotations, its end-to-end results cannot establish that the visual narratives are causally grounded in the cited chart regions.

\paragraph{L5. Sentiment coverage and attribution.}
Strict cutoff filtering prevents future and undated articles from entering a historical decision. Coverage can nevertheless be sparse and uneven across firms, particularly for smaller or less news-intensive equities. The three-symbol ablation finds a positive independent sentiment contribution only for VNM and a negative factor--sentiment interaction for VCB. This heterogeneity limits any broad causal claim about Vietnamese news sentiment.

\subsection{Future Work}

\paragraph{F1. Larger and longer evaluation.}
The most important extension is a preregistered study over a broader universe such as the VN100 and multiple market regimes. More test points would increase per-symbol power, support sector-level analysis, and permit out-of-time validation rather than repeated analysis of the same recent period.

\paragraph{F2. Stronger baselines and multiplicity control.}
Future experiments should add random-walk, logistic, tree-based, and sequence-model baselines on the identical information set. A time-ordered nested design should repeat normalisation, IC orientation, and top-five selection wholly inside each inner training fold, then score the frozen selected bundle on an untouched outer interval \cite{varma2006bias}. White's Reality Check, false-discovery-rate control, or the Deflated Sharpe Ratio should additionally account for searching across 85 candidates. Selection frequency, IC-sign agreement, and rank correlation across rolling calibration windows should be published rather than reporting only the winning factors.

\paragraph{F3. Execution and portfolio realism.}
A portfolio simulator should model lot sizes, taxes, dynamic spreads, liquidity limits, partial fills, and market impact, and should allocate capital jointly across symbols. Confidence gates and risk-based sizing can then be assessed without conflating forecasting accuracy with all-in binary allocation.

\paragraph{F4. Component reliability.}
Repeated hosted-model runs should quantify decision variance and archive provider revision metadata whenever available. Vision faithfulness should be tested with two matched interventions: preserve global trend while changing only the local candlestick evidence, and preserve the local pattern while swapping the trend confounder. Directional changes should then be compared with rule-implied changes and human local-evidence annotations, rather than scored only against the future return. Sentiment should likewise be evaluated against a Vietnamese financial-domain test set. The observed negative VCB interaction also motivates an explicit conflict-resolution mechanism between factor and sentiment evidence.

\paragraph{F5. Per-factor translation audit.}
Each registered adaptation should be profiled by horizon-specific IC decay, autocorrelation at several lags, sign persistence, exposure turnover, and net performance after the same two-leg costs used by the account simulator. Diagnostics should be stratified by symbol and market regime and compared with simple price/volume baselines. Factors whose orientation changes frequently or whose apparent IC disappears before $L=3$ should be excluded by a preregistered stability rule rather than retained through ad hoc interpretation.

\subsection{Broader Impact}

Localized financial AI can broaden research beyond English-language, highly liquid markets, but automated use in a shallow market may amplify noise and herding. The framework should therefore be treated as decision-support research rather than trading advice. Reproducible releases must preserve model and data provenance while respecting news copyright, provider terms, and privacy obligations.
